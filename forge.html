The gap between built and handed over

A commissioning programme runs on three unchecked assumptions.

Each one feels fine on the programme call. Each one is why handover dates move in the last eight weeks, when there is nothing left to give.

PROMISEUnverified

Dates set on faith

CX start dates are declared off construction completeness that nobody has verified at asset level. "The hall will be ready" is a promise made upstream and inherited downstream — no one walked it, asset by asset, to confirm it. FORGE does.

CASCADEAt risk

One slip, silent

An accelerator node slips a week. Downstream, its L3 live test, the integrated L4, the block's L5 IST and two H2C milestones move with it — quietly. By the time it lands on a status deck, the date it threatens has already gone.

SCOREKEEPINGUnfalsifiable

"On track" proves nothing

Nobody keeps score on their own forecasts. Last month's confident "on track" is never checked against what actually happened, so it can never be wrong. FORGE locks every forecast before the outcome — the record can't be edited after the fact.

The wedge · construction → commissioning

Make the CX start date a confirmed date, not a hope.

Today, CX readiness is declared off construction completeness that nobody verified at asset level. A percentage rolls up a hierarchy, someone signs a date, and the whole commissioning programme is planned against it.

FORGE verifies it where it actually lives — at the asset. Construction status is versioned, owner-stamped and evidence-backed: a photo, a signed check, a clean certificate against each step. When FORGE says an asset is ready for CX, that claim has a name and a timestamp behind it.

So the CX start date stops being an upstream promise and becomes a downstream fact you can plan on.
